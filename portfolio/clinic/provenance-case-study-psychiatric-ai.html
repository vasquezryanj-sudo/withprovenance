Case study | Healthcare AI

Psychiatric AI Documentation Startup

A pre-launch tool that transcribes clinician dictation, generates structured clinical notes, and evaluates those notes against insurance payer documents to support prior authorization. It handles PHI, connects to payer systems, and shapes patient care decisions in one of the highest-stakes settings in clinical AI.

Sector
Healthcare AI, psychiatric software
Engagement
April 2026
Scope
Full AI and data governance policy and compliance
Stage
Pre-production, design, deployment, and maintenance

The founding team needed an honest governance assessment before any real patient data entered the system. Psychiatric patients are among the most vulnerable in clinical care, so every design decision carried heightened ethical and legal weight. A TRACE assessment surfaced the pre-launch gaps, and the engagement expanded into a full five-pillar framework built to carry the product from pre-production through deployment and maintenance.

TRACE Framework — Before & After

Before Provenance
After Provenance
TTransparency

No formal AI disclosure to patients or clinicians. Stakeholders had no visibility into when or how the model was contributing to the clinical note. Patients had no means of understanding what was generated versus documented by their provider.

Mandatory AI disclosure on every output. Patient-facing education built into the product to explain what the model does and how. Clinician-selected payer documents constrain and define model scope, making the boundaries of AI involvement explicit at every step.

RRobustness

No incident response plan. Change control was informal and undocumented. Monitoring was minimal with no defined alerting thresholds, failure protocols, or escalation paths.

Contained cloud pipeline with defined monitoring thresholds. Formal incident response protocol and change control procedures written and approved prior to pilot expansion. System failure no longer an unplanned event.

AAccountability & Explainability

Explainability logs collapsed model output, auto-populated fields, and clinician edits into a single undifferentiated record. The source of any given content in a note was indeterminate and could not be attributed after the fact.

Specific prompt stacks written so that every piece of generated content is identifiable by source. Clinician dictation, model inference, and payer policy evaluation remain distinguishable at review. Mandatory attestation on every finalized note.

CCompliance

HIPAA obligations only partially mapped. A critical vendor agreement gap left PHI exposure unaddressed. No alignment with cyber insurance underwriting requirements had been attempted.

Full HIPAA obligation mapping complete. Top vendor agreement gap identified, prioritized, and closed before pilot. Compliance posture simultaneously structured to satisfy cyber insurance underwriting — one gap, two audiences addressed.

EEthics & Harm Reduction

No equity assessment. No adversarial testing. The particular vulnerability of psychiatric patients was not formally addressed in product design, documentation, or any governance artifact.

Absence of proprietary fine-tuning used to limit bias exposure by design. Equity assessment and adversarial testing scoped and scheduled before pilot expansion. Psychiatric patient vulnerability formally integrated into the governance framework as a first-order constraint.

“Working through the governance framework before we deployed gave us a level of confidence we would not have had otherwise. Responsible AI governance became integral to how we thought about what we were building, not just how we documented it.”

Founder, psychiatric AI documentation startup
